Question: Is acral peeling skin syndrome inherited ?

Before fine-tuning:
Yes, acral peeling skin syndrome is an inherited condition. It is caused by mutations in the filaggrin gene (FLG) located on chromosome 1. These mutations lead to a deficiency in the production of filaggrin, a protein important for maintaining the skin barrier. This deficiency results in dry, scaly, and peeling skin, particularly on the hands and feet.

After fine-tuning:
Acral peeling skin syndrome is inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern. This means one copy of the altered gene in each cell is sufficient to cause the disorder. The altered gene is located on chromosome 17.

Reference answer:
This condition is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern, which means both copies of the gene in each cell have mutations. The parents of an individual with an autosomal recessive condition each carry one copy of the mutated gene, but they typically do not show signs and symptoms of the condition.

Question: Is distal hereditary motor neuropathy, type V inherited ?

Before fine-tuning:
Yes, distal hereditary motor neuropathy, type V (DMN-V) is an inherited condition. It is caused by mutations in the PMP22 gene, which is passed down through families. This genetic mutation affects the function of the peripheral nerves, leading to muscle weakness and wasting, primarily in the hands and feet.

After fine-tuning:
This condition is inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern, which means one copy of the altered gene in each cell is sufficient to cause the disorder. The altered gene is located on chromosome 17.

Reference answer:
This condition is inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern, which means one copy of the altered gene in each cell is sufficient to cause the disorder. Some people who have the altered gene never develop the condition, a situation known as reduced penetrance.
